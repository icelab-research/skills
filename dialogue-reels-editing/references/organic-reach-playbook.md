# Organic Reach Playbook

Virality cannot be guaranteed. Treat each edit as a falsifiable hypothesis about a specific cold audience, then learn from retention and sharing data.

## Evidence hierarchy

Use sources in this order:

1. current platform guidance and the account's own analytics
2. the show's past posts, especially unusually strong or weak retention curves
3. comparable niche creators and outlier posts
4. open-source editing skills and practitioner heuristics

Platform guidance describes ranking inputs, not a guaranteed recipe. Advertising playbooks may inspire craft, but do not present them as proof of organic ranking.

## The first-frame contract

The first spoken line, first visual, on-screen headline, title, and cover must promise the same thing. Open on content rather than a logo animation or a multi-second cover card.

Before editing, write three to five truthful hook candidates:

- **Contradiction:** `The best infra founders are not all good at the same thing.`
- **Unresolved question:** `Should podcast hosts ever introduce themselves?`
- **Specific insider claim:** `Why WorkOS and Neon win for completely different reasons.`
- **Consequential mistake:** `The infra content we grew up with no longer works for younger engineers.`
- **Human moment:** `Two infrastructure people try to name a podcast live.`

Choose the shortest hook that is fully paid off by the available footage. Do not manufacture stakes the speakers never establish.

## Retention structure

Use a simple spine:

1. **Hook:** state or show the tension immediately.
2. **Setup:** provide only the context needed to understand the claim.
3. **Escalation:** add a distinction, surprise, disagreement, or consequence.
4. **Payoff:** deliver the promised answer, joke, framework, or reveal.
5. **Exit:** stop on the strongest complete line; do not trail into housekeeping.

Add a visual or editorial change only when attention or meaning changes. Useful pattern interrupts include an active-speaker cut, a precise punch-in, a diagram, a sourced screenshot, an original meme, or a restrained sound cue. Constant motion is not retention strategy.

## Clip mining for technical podcasts

Prioritize moments with at least two of these qualities:

- a named technology, company, founder, or concrete engineering practice
- a surprising opinion with a defensible reason
- a clean distinction or framework, such as `there are two categories`
- a mistake, failure, tradeoff, or lesson learned
- disagreement, persuasion, or a changed mind
- an awkward, funny, or highly relatable human moment
- a bridge between deep infrastructure and a younger or broader audience
- a career or hiring insight with a clear consequence
- a sentence viewers might send to a colleague

Reject excerpts that need extensive missing context, repeat the long episode without a payoff, or rely on a misleading headline.

## Platform-aware testing

Keep the core story stable and vary one element at a time:

- opening spoken line
- first-frame headline
- clip length or exit point
- cover/title packaging
- one relevant visual proof or meme
- caption phrasing, without changing meaning

For Instagram, eligible accounts can use Trial Reels to show a test to non-followers first and review views, likes, comments, and shares before wider distribution. For YouTube, inspect audience retention at key moments and whether the opening delivers on the title/thumbnail promise. For TikTok, consider watch time, completion, shares, relevant captions/sounds, and audience fit together rather than optimizing a single metric.

Do not rely on `#FYP`, bulk hashtags, copyrighted trend audio, or rapid cuts with no narrative purpose.

## Post-publication learning loop

Record each variant and review after the platform has had time to distribute it:

- viewed versus swiped away, when available
- average view duration and percentage viewed
- first major retention drop
- completion and rewatch signals
- shares or sends per reach
- saves per reach
- comments that reveal what viewers thought the clip was about
- profile visits, follows, or long-episode clicks

Translate the result into one reusable rule. Do not generalize from one post when topic, timing, audience, and packaging all changed together.
